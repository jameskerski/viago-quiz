## L–M. Must every single-select remain one option per color?

No psychometric or software principle requires every future item to contain exactly one option per color. The rule is operationally convenient: every answered item offers equal nominal opportunity and produces simple additive scoring. It can also force unnatural fourth answers and weaken recognition.

Research recommendation: keep four-way balanced items as the current scoring baseline, but separately prototype evidence-typed items. A one-color indicator, two-color discriminator, or contra-evidence candidate requires an explicit item scoring vector, calibrated weights, opportunity normalization by color, missing-opportunity rules, and validation for response attractiveness and information value. Contra evidence must remain non-scoring until independently authorized. Raw totals would no longer be comparable unless normalized to earned points divided by available positive opportunity (with versioned rounding/tie rules). Do not mix architectures in a live bank without simulation and OWNER approval.
