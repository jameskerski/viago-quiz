# VIAGO V2 Interim Question-Bank Coverage Analysis

Status: `OWNER_REVIEW_COVERAGE_ANALYSIS`  
Governing model: `VIAGO_BEHAVIORAL_COLOR_MODEL_V1_0`  
Scope: 151 active legacy questions + 40 OWNER-reviewed, non-production candidates  
Production impact: **NONE**

## Executive Summary

- **The 40 candidates materially improve the bank, especially the single-select pool.** The review-only combined bank would contain 191 questions: 140 Likert and 51 single-select. If all 20 new single-select candidates were later activated under the existing 25-single selector, the expected shared single-select questions between two attempts would fall from 20.16 to 12.25.
- **The additions improve weak leadership and situational coverage but remain too work/team concentrated.** Twenty-one of the 40 candidates are explicitly work, customer, team, or professional contexts; only 11 are clearly ordinary-life/community contexts. The active audit's broad `personal-preference` label cannot be treated as proof of genuine ordinary-life diversity.
- **The 40 are not free of semantic concentration.** The strongest reconsideration clusters are group-choice convergence (`C01-S-07` / `C02-S-15`), disruption/change response (`C01-S-03` / `C02-S-03` / `C02-S-08`), and several Likert concepts that sit close to active legacy wording. These are review flags, not automatic rejections.
- **About 220 remains a sensible coverage checkpoint, not a success metric; 280 should not yet be a commitment.** A gap-led Cohort 03 of roughly 12–20 candidates is preferable to automatically adding 29. Reassess coverage, redundancy, field performance, and repeat exposure before deciding whether another tranche is justified.

## D. Color Gaps

- **Yellow is the lowest combined opportunity count (83), but not by enough to justify quota writing.** Its candidate Likert count is only 3, so the best Yellow additions would measure historically established loyalty, continuity, support, and harmony through balanced tradeoffs in ordinary life—not generic kindness, conflict skill, or introversion.
- **Red has improved substantially through leadership and action ownership.** Additional Red leadership items risk repeating decisive-action language; future Red coverage should prefer underrepresented ordinary-life achievement, independent follow-through, or risk tradeoffs.
- **Blue has adequate total coverage, but novelty/energy language is becoming concentrated.** Future Blue items should explore expressive connection, experience orientation, spontaneous possibility, and social energy in distinct contexts without reducing Blue to sociability or excitement.
- **Green remains highest (91).** Future Green items should be gap-led—evidence, precision, structure, and risk control in underrepresented settings—not more generic planning, intelligence, organization, or process preference.

## E. Behavioral-Domain Gaps

Recommended next measurement priorities are:

1. **Independent follow-through and recovery** without equating personality with responsibility or competence.
2. **Problem framing and learning entry** where color preference is expressed through motive, not intelligence or a claimed learning style.
3. **Detail/accuracy tradeoffs** balanced against equally reasonable speed, possibility, or relational priorities.
4. **Stability/security and continuity** in household, purchasing, travel, friendship, and personal-plan settings.
5. **Communication preference** outside customer/team situations: unfamiliar social settings, family coordination, friendship, and community participation.
6. **Conflict and boundaries** only where every response is mature and useful; avoid making Yellow uniquely caring, Red uniquely courageous, or Green uniquely reasonable.

Leadership is no longer the clearest numerical gap after six new candidates. Risk/change and generic decision-making should be deprioritized until the existing candidate clusters are resolved.

## F. Context Gaps

Of the 40 candidates, **21 are explicitly work/business/team/professional**, 11 are clearly ordinary-life or community, and 8 are cross-context or unspecified. Team alone accounts for 8 candidates and work/business for 6.

The next tranche should strongly favor:

- family and household decisions beyond routines;
- friendships and one-to-one relationships;
- purchasing and resource choices;
- personal projects and hobbies;
- group recreation;
- unfamiliar social situations;
- everyday practical problems;
- travel beyond disruption response;
- community participation without work-team framing.

The active audit labels 89 items `personal-preference`, but that broad bucket does not establish concrete life context. Future audits should separate truly situated ordinary-life evidence from decontextualized self-description.

## G. Semantic Overlap / Duplication Within the 40 Candidates

### High-priority review clusters

1. **`C01-S-07` and `C02-S-15`: group-choice convergence.** Both ask what contribution comes naturally when a group cannot settle on a direction, with closely parallel Red narrowing, Blue possibility, Yellow support, and Green criteria/logistics options. Keep at most one unchanged; the other needs a meaningfully different construct or setting.
2. **`C01-S-03`, `C02-S-03`, and `C02-S-08`: disruption/change response.** The contexts differ (personal plan, group travel, community event), but the same progress/possibility/commitment/clarity architecture repeats. Context diversity alone may not provide distinct measurement value.
3. **`C01-S-01` and `C02-S-07`: urgent first-response architecture.** These are distinguishable as unowned incident versus customer complaint, but their option motives are strongly parallel. Retain only if later review confirms useful context generalization rather than repetition.

### Moderate concentration

- `C02-S-01`, `C02-S-06`, and `C02-S-12` all use a four-color “what contribution/help comes first” structure in organized-group settings.
- `C01-L-R-01`, `C01-L-R-02`, `C01-L-R-03`, and `C01-L-R-04` share the `decisive-action` family. Their tradeoffs differ, but the family is already concentrated.
- `C01-L-G-01`, `C01-L-G-02`, and `C01-L-G-03` share `evidence-before-action`; retain only with demonstrably distinct conditions.

Lexical cosine was used only as a screening aid. The strongest candidate pair was `C01-S-07` / `C02-S-15` (0.309); semantic review, not the score, produced the recommendations above.

The clearest legacy-proximity flags are:

- **`C01-L-B-03`** (finding something interesting after a last-minute plan change) is close to active single `16d86da0-614b-4c2f-a935-51e08ab09baa` and active novelty item `a9e9ac76-174c-4b12-b3ea-32edcf609761`. It may still improve wording quality, but adds limited new semantic coverage.
- **`C01-L-R-05`** (mistake recovery toward the next decision) is close to active single `d60128b7-cef9-42f3-8841-3b4d024cb71b`. Its ownership/recovery framing is better focused, but should be tested as a replacement-quality item rather than counted as a wholly new construct.
- **`C01-L-G-01`** (clarifying incomplete instructions before starting) overlaps active single `4ec48535-9510-4e86-9ee7-a6ea509af280`. The candidate is more precise, yet its incremental coverage is modest.

This can provide useful bidirectional evidence, but it should be treated as the same tradeoff family rather than independent domain breadth.

These findings do not revoke OWNER candidate approval. They identify where activation review should distinguish **new coverage**, **higher-quality replacement**, and **deliberate repeated measurement**.

## I. Current Single-Select Depth and Projected Repeat Exposure

Under a uniform 25-of-pool assumption:

| Repeat-exposure measure | Active 31-single pool | Combined 51-single pool |
|---|---:|---:|
| Chance a particular single appears in an attempt | 80.6% | 49.0% |
| Expected shared singles between two attempts | 20.16 | 12.25 |
| Expected pairwise Jaccard similarity | 67.6% | 32.5% |

For Likert, the analogous per-item appearance probability falls from 20.8% (25 of 120) to 17.9% (25 of 140).

This projection demonstrates substantial repeat-exposure relief, but it is not a selector simulation. It assumes uniform eligibility and ignores color/domain balancing, exclusions, prior-attempt avoidance, and any future activation rules. Actual selector testing remains required before activation.

## J. Recommended Content Brief for Cohort 03

Do not target a fixed color quota. Build a **small, gap-led tranche of roughly 12–20 review-only candidates**, with a majority single-select, after resolving the high-priority overlap clusters.

The brief should require:

- at least two-thirds of situational items in clearly ordinary-life/community contexts;
- emphasis on independent follow-through, problem framing, detail/accuracy tradeoffs, stability/continuity, and non-work communication;
- no additional generic change-disruption, stalled-group-choice, team-launch, or “contribution comes first” variants unless they measure a new motive;
- deliberate Yellow items grounded in loyalty/continuity/support/harmony without virtue framing;
- Blue breadth beyond novelty/excitement;
- Red breadth beyond decisive-action;
- Green breadth beyond generic planning/process;
- explicit `NEW_COVERAGE`, `REPLACEMENT_QUALITY`, or `REPEATED_MEASUREMENT` intent for every candidate;
- response desirability balance and semantic-family comparison against both active and development banks before OWNER review.

## K. Do the ~220 Then ~280 Targets Still Make Sense?

**About 220 remains useful as a checkpoint, not an objective.** The current combined review bank is 191, so reaching 220 would require 29 more items. The evidence does not justify automatically filling all 29 in one cohort: the next useful work is narrower and gap-led.

**About 280 should be deferred.** The single-select exposure problem is already materially improved at 51 potential singles, while semantic duplication is beginning to appear. After a modest Cohort 03 and overlap cleanup, VIAGO should evaluate:

1. domain/context coverage against the governed model;
2. selector simulation and repeat-attempt experience;
3. cognitive interviews and OWNER review;
4. item response distributions and color-score contribution after controlled activation;
5. reliability and redundancy within semantic families.

Only demonstrated gaps should authorize expansion beyond the ~220 checkpoint. A 280-question bank could ultimately be useful, but the current evidence does not establish that it is necessary or that quantity would improve measurement.

## Caveats and Assumptions

- Active domain/context labels come from the forensic audit and include `INFERENCE_REQUIRES_OWNER_REVIEW`; they are not governed scoring doctrine.
- Single-select exposure is a mathematical projection under uniform sampling, not observed production behavior or a full implementation of selector constraints.
- Lexical TF-IDF similarity can miss conceptual duplicates and surface wording similarities that are not true construct duplicates. All final overlap findings require human semantic review.
- Candidate counts describe approved development candidates, not activation readiness.
