This is the OWNER-approved canonical semantic meaning of Red, Blue, Yellow, and Green inside VIAGO. It is richer than a result summary but deliberately avoids claiming more than the preserved VIAGO evidence supports. Questions do not define this model. This model defines what questions are allowed to measure.

## How to read the evidence labels

- **HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED** — stated or strongly represented in preserved VIAGO result content.
- **OWNER_GOVERNED** — an explicit OWNER rule or interpretation adopted for this proposal.
- **SUPPORTED_INFERENCE** — a reasonable extension of established meaning that may guide research, but is not scoring authority.
- **NOT_ESTABLISHED** — a tempting assumption VIAGO currently lacks authority to use.

Only historically established and OWNER-governed claims may be considered for scoring authority, and only after separate question/content approval and implementation authorization. Model approval does not promote supported inference or authorize runtime scoring changes.

# A. RED

## Identity

**Driver / Achiever.** Red restores progress and secures outcomes through decisive ownership and momentum. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Core preference and motivation

Red is naturally drawn toward progress, challenge, competition, achievement, visible results, and forward motion. Targets, scoreboards, responsibility, recognition, and the chance to advance can energize Red. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Legitimate indicators

- Choosing a workable direction quickly when delay threatens progress. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- Taking ownership, setting the next move, pushing a deadline, negotiating, or pursuing a milestone. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- Speaking directly when the underlying purpose is closure, accountability, progress, or outcome. Directness alone is insufficient. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**

## Behavioral pattern

- **Decision:** timely closure and action over extended deliberation when progress is at stake. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Communication:** concise, candid, and outcome-focused is consistent with Red, but is partly inferred. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**
- **Work:** goals, milestones, deadlines, standards, and visible results. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Relationships:** may place the objective above relational comfort and may care about visible competence or recognition. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Leadership:** readily assumes responsibility, direction, and pace. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Conflict:** can become blunt, impatient, dismissive, or steamroll others while pursuing closure. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Pressure:** narrows attention to the objective, decides, and takes control. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Change:** likely supports change that restores progress or improves results. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**
- **Risk:** an inherently aggressive or high risk appetite is not established. **[NOT_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Planning:** enough structure to drive action is plausible; exhaustive Red planning doctrine is not established. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**

## Strengths and overextensions

Strengths include decisiveness, momentum, ambition, ownership, responsibility, and result focus. Overextensions include impatience, premature closure, weak listening after deciding, dismissiveness, status sensitivity, and steamrolling. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Growth behaviors

Listening longer, developing people, consulting others, and preserving morale are Red growth behaviors. They must not be interpreted as evidence that someone is not Red. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Must not independently score Red

Directness, courage, competence, responsibility, holding a leadership title, meeting a deadline, or correcting a mistake. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**

# B. BLUE

## Identity

**Energizer / Explorer.** Blue creates energy and connection through expression, novelty, freedom, and shared experience. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Core preference and motivation

Blue is naturally drawn toward experience, interaction, stimulation, novelty, possibility, spontaneity, freedom, fun, and a socially rich life. Routine and micromanagement drain energy. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Legitimate indicators

- Bringing expressive energy, optimism, conversation, or enthusiasm to a group. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- Engaging readily with novelty and losing energy when work becomes repetitive. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- Connecting broadly through shared experience and social energy. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Behavioral pattern

- **Decision:** may follow interest, intuition, possibility, or spontaneous momentum and undercheck consequences. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Communication:** outgoing, expressive, animated, conversational, and audience-responsive. Storytelling specifically is not established as a defining mechanism. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED / NOT_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Work:** energetic and creative starts; simple structure may be needed for repetitive follow-through. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Relationships:** broad connection through warmth, openness, interaction, and shared experience. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Leadership:** energizes participation and prefers engaging, humane, non-micromanaging leadership. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Conflict:** habitual lightening, reframing, or avoidance of heaviness is not established. **[NOT_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Pressure:** improvisation and possibility generation are plausible; scattering or impulsivity may increase. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**
- **Change:** novelty and possibility can energize Blue; repetitive implementation can drain it. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Risk:** impulsivity can produce poorly examined risk, but a general risk appetite is not established. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**
- **Planning:** flexible direction and room to improvise, supported by simple consistency systems. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Strengths and overextensions

Strengths include social energy, optimism, adaptability, creativity, expression, connection, and possibility generation. Overextensions include impulsivity, time blindness, distraction, novelty dependence, and weak repetitive follow-through. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Growth behaviors

Checking consequences, using simple systems, sustaining repetitive work, and completing after the excitement fades are growth behaviors, not evidence against Blue. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Must not independently score Blue

Friendliness, storytelling skill, public-speaking skill, creativity, adaptability, sociability, or enjoying people. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**

# C. YELLOW

## Identity

**Stabilizer / Loyalist.** Yellow protects people, values, trust, harmony, and durable relationships through loyal support. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Core preference and motivation

Yellow is naturally drawn toward values, fairness, ethical treatment, belonging, relational safety, harmony, support, loyalty, and dependable connection. It is energized when people feel included, cared for, treated fairly, and able to rely on one another. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Legitimate indicators

- Noticing exclusion, discomfort, unmet support needs, or relational strain. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- Investing loyally, carrying relational load, protecting belonging, or supporting continuity over time. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- Quieter, compassionate, caring, supportive, or softer interpersonal behavior only when it connects meaningfully to loyalty, harmony, values, or support. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**

## Behavioral pattern

- **Decision:** considers people impact, commitments, trust, values, and fairness before closure. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Communication:** attentive, caring, tactful, supportive, and inclusive. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**
- **Work:** dependable stabilization of the group, sometimes by quietly carrying too much. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Relationships:** durable trust, honored commitments, belonging, loyalty, and sometimes staying too long. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Leadership:** does not characteristically crave authority, cares who leads and how, and may hesitate when leadership requires rupture. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Conflict:** often avoids or softens conflict, self-silences, or enables behavior to preserve harmony. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Pressure:** carries relational load until resentment, exhaustion, or burnout can develop. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Change:** considering people, commitments, safety, and continuity is plausible but not historical scoring authority. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**
- **Risk:** neither a generally low risk appetite nor self-sacrificial risk is established as VIAGO doctrine. **[NOT_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Planning:** dependable coordination because others rely on the plan is plausible but not established. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**

## Strengths and overextensions

Strengths include loyalty, dependability, support, integrity, emotional intuition, inclusion, trust building, and values awareness. Overextensions include conflict avoidance, self-silencing, enabling, overcommitment, reluctance to lead, resentment, and burnout. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Growth behaviors — critical rule

Direct conversations, assertiveness, courageous confrontation, healthy boundaries, and leading through relational discomfort are learnable Yellow growth behaviors. They are **not** core Yellow scoring evidence. A mature Yellow may be excellent at all of them; so may any other color. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**

## Must not independently score Yellow

- Directness, confrontation, advocacy, boundary-setting, kindness, fairness, compassion, or support. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**
- Introversion, quietness, or soft-spokenness. These can also occur in other colors, especially Green. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**

# D. GREEN

## Identity

**Analyst / Planner.** Green creates clarity and dependable systems through evidence, logic, precision, structure, and risk awareness. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Core preference and motivation

Green is naturally drawn toward clarity, logic, evidence, accuracy, predictability, defined systems, and clear expectations. Understanding the problem, checking assumptions, reducing uncertainty, and creating a reliable plan or process can energize Green. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Legitimate indicators

- Researching, analyzing, questioning assumptions, asking for specifics, and checking consequences before commitment. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- Creating structure, guidelines, plans, processes, standards, or quality checks. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- Accepting a real speed cost because verification, coherence, or risk control matters. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**

## Behavioral pattern

- **Decision:** defines the problem, separates evidence from assumption, and seeks enough information before commitment. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Communication:** measured, precise, question-led, qualification-aware, and focused on specifics is plausible from the established core. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**
- **Work:** systems, steps, standards, contingencies, and checks for reliable execution. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Relationships:** inherent social selectivity or reliability-based bonding is not established. **[NOT_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Leadership:** leadership through expertise and standards is plausible but not established VIAGO doctrine. **[NOT_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Conflict:** moving toward facts, definitions, evidence, and process is a supported inference, not yet scoring authority. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**
- **Pressure:** adds structure and verification but may slow, rigidify, or appear cold or critical. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Change:** seeks rationale, implications, and an executable transition. **[SUPPORTED_INFERENCE]**
- **Risk:** checks consequences and reduces avoidable uncertainty. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**
- **Planning:** plans, systems, clear expectations, standards, and quality checks. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Strengths and overextensions

Strengths include analysis, thoroughness, precision, planning, consistency, calm logic, systems thinking, and risk awareness. Overextensions include analysis paralysis, rigidity, excessive certainty-seeking, delay, and appearing cold or critical. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Growth behaviors

Acting before every unknown is resolved, tolerating imperfect information, and engaging emotional or relational context are growth behaviors, not evidence against Green. **[HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED]**

## Must not independently score Green

Accuracy, intelligence, noticing a missing instruction, checking work, being reasonable, or making a careful high-stakes choice. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**

# E. Four-color discriminator matrix

Surface behavior is never enough. The item must reveal why the person prefers the behavior or what meaningful tradeoff they accept.

| Same surface behavior | Red motive | Blue motive | Yellow motive | Green motive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acts quickly | Restore progress, control, or closure | Follow novelty, energy, or possibility | Meet an immediate people/support need | Evidence threshold has already been met |
| Takes leadership | Own direction and results | Create enthusiasm and participation | Protect people, trust, or values | Create standards, clarity, or risk control |
| Supports someone | Enable performance or progress | Create connection or shared energy | Sustain care, loyalty, fairness, or belonging | Provide reliable information, structure, or a solution |
| Challenges a decision | Improve outcome, pace, ownership, or accountability | Keep possibility, freedom, or participation alive | Protect people, fairness, trust, or commitments | Correct weak evidence, logic, assumptions, or process |
| Plans carefully | Secure objectives and milestones | Preserve room for an engaging possibility | Keep commitments dependable for people | Reduce uncertainty and create a coherent system |
| Communicates directly | Drive closure or accountability | Express an immediate idea | Learned behavior; not Yellow evidence by itself | State a precise conclusion or correction |
| Appears quiet | No color meaning without motive | No color meaning without motive | May accompany support/harmony; insufficient alone | May accompany analysis/observation; insufficient alone |
| Changes course | Restore results or momentum | Pursue novelty or possibility | Protect people, trust, or continuity | Respond to stronger evidence or a safer plan |

The rows above are mostly **SUPPORTED_INFERENCE** and are intended to guide future item research. The directness and quietness rules are **OWNER_GOVERNED**.

## Pairwise confusion zones

- **Red / Blue:** both can move quickly and energize action. Red seeks outcome, control, and closure; Blue seeks novelty, freedom, expression, and possibility.
- **Red / Yellow:** both can advocate or lead. Red prioritizes progress/accountability; Yellow prioritizes people/trust/values.
- **Red / Green:** both can look competent and responsible. Red favors pace and closure; Green favors evidence and coherence.
- **Blue / Yellow:** both value people. Blue favors broad interaction and shared experience; Yellow favors durable loyalty and relational safety.
- **Blue / Green:** both can create ideas. Blue expands possibility and energy; Green tests coherence and implications.
- **Yellow / Green:** both may appear quiet or stability-seeking. Yellow protects relational continuity; Green protects clarity and predictability.

# F. Universal and nonqualifying behaviors

Kindness, fairness, courage, competence, responsibility, honesty, accountability, accuracy, emotional maturity, and good communication are universal virtues—not personality colors. A valid question must reveal a preference, motive, or meaningful tradeoff rather than ask whether the respondent is a good, capable, or mature person. **[OWNER_GOVERNED]**

Additional universal cautions:

- Directness and leadership do not automatically mean Red.
- Friendliness, creativity, and communication skill do not automatically mean Blue.
- Kindness, compassion, quietness, advocacy, and boundaries do not automatically mean Yellow.
- Intelligence, accuracy, and careful work do not automatically mean Green.

# G. Supported inferences not yet scoring authority

- Red communication tends to be concise/outcome-focused; Red favors enough planning to move.
- Blue improvises under pressure; impulsivity can create poorly examined risk.
- Yellow evaluates change through people and continuity; Yellow may coordinate dependably because others rely on the plan.
- Green communicates precisely, approaches conflict through facts/process, and wants rationale and transition detail before change.
- The motivational distinctions in the discriminator matrix are research hypotheses until separately validated and approved for scoring.

# H. Not-established claims to avoid

- Red is inherently high-risk, aggressive-risk, or entitled to bypass consensus.
- Blue is inherently story-first or characteristically lightens/avoids conflict.
- Yellow is defined by introversion, quietness, soft-spokenness, direct advocacy, boundaries, or a general low-risk style.
- Green is inherently socially selective, emotionally detached, or a leader only through expertise and standards.
- Any universal virtue or professional competency is independently diagnostic of a color.
