# VIAGO V2 color-model foundation and Cohort 01 recalibration

> **OWNER REVIEW ONLY.** This document defines a proposed behavioral foundation and re-evaluates proposal content. It does not approve, publish, score, migrate, or activate a question.

## Decision standard

A behavior supports a color only when its recurring motive or tradeoff distinguishes that color from the other three. Competence, courage, kindness, accuracy, responsibility, and reasonableness do not independently qualify.

A color is not a list of admirable skills. The same visible behavior can arise from different motives. A useful item must expose a preference, attention pattern, or tradeoff that makes one color more likely than the other three. When a question merely asks whether someone is competent, considerate, responsible, courageous, accurate, or reasonable, it is rejected or revised.

### Stable conclusion

- **Red:** outcome and agency priority.
- **Blue:** stimulation, expression, freedom, and possibility priority.
- **Yellow:** people, values, loyalty, fairness, and relational-safety priority.
- **Green:** clarity, evidence, predictability, accuracy, and risk-control priority.

## Red behavioral definition

### Core motivations

Progress, agency, challenge, visible results, achievement, and maintaining momentum.

### Decision style

Chooses a workable direction quickly, accepts imperfect information, and values closure when delay threatens progress.

### Communication style

Direct, concise, outcome-focused, candid, and willing to challenge.

### Relationship orientation

Respects strength, competence, autonomy, and accountability; may prioritize the objective over relational comfort.

### Conflict response

Moves toward conflict to resolve, decide, or restore accountability; can become blunt or impatient.

### Response to pressure

Narrows attention to the objective, assumes control, sets the next move, and may discount consultation.

### Leadership / followership

Readily owns direction and pace; follows credible leadership when authority is clear and progress remains possible.

### Approach to change

Supports change that improves results or restores momentum; may change course before consensus is complete.

### Approach to risk

Accepts calculated or even aggressive risk when upside, challenge, or control justifies it.

### Planning / execution

Plans around goals, milestones, and action; prefers sufficient structure to move rather than exhaustive preparation.

### Common strengths

decisiveness; momentum; ownership; goal focus; comfort with responsibility

### Common overextensions

impatience; steamrolling; premature closure; status sensitivity; listening only until a decision is made

### Frequently confused behaviors

Blue may act quickly for novelty or excitement; Red acts for progress and outcome.; Green may look responsible or competent; Red distinguishes itself by decision ownership and pace.; Yellow may confront an issue to protect trust or fairness; Red confronts to resolve, decide, or restore accountability.

### What does not independently qualify

being competent; being courageous; holding a leadership title; telling the truth; fixing a mistake; meeting a deadline

## Blue behavioral definition

### Core motivations

Stimulation, freedom, experience, expression, novelty, possibility, and broad social connection.

### Decision style

Follows interest, possibility, intuition, and social momentum; prefers options that keep energy and freedom alive.

### Communication style

Expressive, conversational, animated, story-oriented, improvisational, and responsive to audience energy.

### Relationship orientation

Connects quickly and broadly through openness, shared experience, warmth, and emotional expressiveness.

### Conflict response

Often tries to reopen conversation, lighten heaviness, improvise a reset, or move toward a more energizing frame; may avoid sustained tension.

### Response to pressure

Improvises, rallies people, generates alternatives, and can become scattered or impulsive when constraints tighten.

### Leadership / followership

Leads through enthusiasm, possibility, and participation; follows engaging leadership but resists micromanagement and dull routine.

### Approach to change

Experiences change as novelty and possibility when freedom remains; loses energy when change becomes repetitive implementation.

### Approach to risk

Accepts risk for experience, opportunity, freedom, or excitement, sometimes without sustained consequence checking.

### Planning / execution

Prefers flexible direction and room to improvise; starts readily and may need simple structure for follow-through.

### Common strengths

social energy; creativity; adaptability; expressive connection; possibility generation

### Common overextensions

impulsivity; time blindness; novelty dependence; weak repetitive follow-through; avoiding heaviness

### Frequently confused behaviors

Red also creates momentum; Blue seeks stimulation/connection while Red seeks outcome/control.; Yellow also values people; Blue favors breadth, expression, and shared experience while Yellow favors loyalty, fairness, and relational safety.; A Green or Red person may be an excellent storyteller; skill alone is not Blue.

### What does not independently qualify

being friendly; being a good public speaker; telling stories effectively; adapting competently; having creative skill; enjoying people

## Yellow behavioral definition

### Core motivations

Fairness, loyalty, belonging, ethical treatment, relational safety, continuity, and dependable mutual support.

### Decision style

Considers effects on people, commitments, trust, and values; accepts slower closure when relational harm feels unresolved.

### Communication style

Attentive, tactful, inclusive, supportive, and sensitive to what others may not be saying.

### Relationship orientation

Invests deeply, honors commitments, protects belonging, and favors durable trust over broad stimulation or transactional success.

### Conflict response

Historically avoids or softens conflict to preserve harmony; mature Yellow directness is color-relevant only when the motive is protecting trust, fairness, or a relationship—not directness by itself.

### Response to pressure

Stabilizes people, carries relational load, preserves continuity, and may overfunction or suppress disagreement until resentment builds.

### Leadership / followership

Leads through service, trust, inclusion, and stewardship; may resist positional authority or hesitate when leadership requires interpersonal rupture.

### Approach to change

Evaluates change through its effect on people, commitments, safety, and continuity.

### Approach to risk

Is cautious when risk could harm people or violate commitments; may accept personal cost to protect the group.

### Planning / execution

Favors dependable coordination and sustainable pace, especially when others rely on the plan.

### Common strengths

loyalty; trust building; empathy; ethical awareness; dependability; inclusion

### Common overextensions

conflict avoidance; enabling; self-silencing; overcommitment; burnout; reluctance to lead through rupture

### Frequently confused behaviors

Blue also connects with people; Yellow emphasizes loyalty and safety while Blue emphasizes energy and shared experience.; Green also prefers stability; Yellow protects people/commitments while Green protects clarity/predictability.; Red or Green can advocate fairly; the Yellow signal is persistent people/values priority when it costs speed, comfort, or advantage.

### What does not independently qualify

being kind; being considerate; supporting someone; advocating for an absent person; setting healthy boundaries; acting fairly when fairness is obviously expected

## Green behavioral definition

### Core motivations

Clarity, evidence, accuracy, predictability, coherence, risk control, and reliable systems.

### Decision style

Defines the problem, separates facts from assumptions, checks consequences, and seeks an evidence threshold before commitment.

### Communication style

Precise, measured, question-led, qualification-aware, and more comfortable with specifics than emotional momentum.

### Relationship orientation

Selective and reliability-oriented; trust grows through consistency, follow-through, and understandable expectations.

### Conflict response

Moves toward facts, definitions, evidence, and process; may detach from emotional content or appear critical.

### Response to pressure

Creates structure, verifies priorities, and protects critical checks; may slow excessively or become rigid when rapid imperfection is required.

### Leadership / followership

Leads through expertise, standards, planning, and risk control; follows when expectations and reasoning are credible.

### Approach to change

Wants rationale, implications, and an executable transition; can support major change once uncertainty is bounded.

### Approach to risk

Reduces avoidable uncertainty, examines downstream effects, and prefers controlled experiments or proven methods.

### Planning / execution

Builds steps, standards, contingencies, and quality checks before or during execution.

### Common strengths

analysis; precision; planning; consistency; risk awareness; systems thinking

### Common overextensions

analysis paralysis; rigidity; overqualification; appearing cold or critical; delaying action for certainty

### Frequently confused behaviors

Red can be highly competent; Green is distinguished by evidence/structure priority rather than outcome pace.; Yellow can prefer stability; Green seeks predictable systems while Yellow seeks relational continuity.; Any color can notice details; Green is indicated when verification and coherence win a real tradeoff against speed, novelty, or harmony.

### What does not independently qualify

being accurate; noticing a missing instruction; being reasonable; checking work; solving a problem; making a careful choice when consequences obviously demand it

## Important overlap and confusion zones

### Action and adaptability: Red vs Blue

Both may act quickly and welcome change. Red is pulled by progress, control, challenge, or outcome. Blue is pulled by novelty, freedom, possibility, or social energy. “Adapts well” alone proves neither.

### People orientation: Blue vs Yellow

Blue seeks broad connection, expression, and shared experience. Yellow protects loyalty, fairness, belonging, and durable trust. Friendliness, empathy, or sociability alone is insufficient.

### Stability: Yellow vs Green

Yellow protects people and commitments; Green protects clarity, reliable systems, and bounded uncertainty. Both may resist change, but for different reasons.

### Responsibility and competence: Red vs Green

Red owns direction and pace; Green owns correctness, evidence, and process integrity. Fixing a problem, noticing missing information, or being responsible is not color-specific without a tradeoff.

### Direct conflict: Red vs mature Yellow

Red directness seeks resolution, decision, or accountability. Yellow directness can protect trust or fairness, but it is often a developed corrective to Yellow conflict avoidance. Directness itself should not be scored Yellow.

### Universal virtues

Courage, kindness, fairness, accuracy, accountability, calmness, and good judgment are not colors. Items using them need competing, equally defensible priorities.

## Places where established VIAGO meaning remains unclear

1. **Yellow directness:** historical VIAGO defines conflict avoidance as an overextension, but does not specify when developed directness becomes evidence of Yellow rather than simply healthy behavior.
2. **Blue adaptability:** historical copy calls Blue adaptable while also describing impulsivity and weak follow-through. The model needs to distinguish attraction to novelty from effective adaptation.
3. **Red accountability versus Green rigor:** corpus items sometimes treat responsibility as Red even when the behavior could reflect Green reliability or universal conscientiousness.
4. **Color versus maturity:** result narratives contain growth behaviors. These should guide coaching, not automatically become scoring indicators.
5. **Green relationship stability:** several Green items describe selective, steady relationships. It is unclear whether this is a core Green construct or predictability expressed in a relationship context.
6. **Yellow fairness:** fairness is central, but obvious ethical conduct creates social-desirability bias unless it competes with speed, advantage, comfort, or loyalty.
